Facts from the official Farthest Frontier guide (farming):
- Fields are 5x5 to 12x12 tiles. Size sets yield and the farmers needed: 1 farmer minimum, up to 7 on 12x12.
- Debris (rocks, trees) must be cleared and the ground tilled first; this halts in freezing weather and
  can take years on difficult terrain. Prefer clean, fertile ground (fertility overlay).
- Rotations run 3 years and repeat. Heavy feeders (wheat in particular) drain fertility; balance them
  with nitrogen-fixing crops (beans, clover) or the field turns barren. Clover needs no harvest and
  spreads farmers' work.
- Each crop prefers a clay/sand mix (up to +10% yield). Weeds and rocks reduce yield; a
  "maintenance" year removes weeds and rocks instead of planting.
- Compost from the Compost Yard gives a fertility bonus at the start of the next rotation.
- Crop diseases (bean wilt, club root, aster yellows, powdery mildew) build up in the soil. Rotate to
  crops a disease does not affect, and do not grow the same crop in neighbouring fields.
Crop parameters in the knowledge pack marked "approximate" are estimates, not game data; prefer the
evaluate_rotation tool over your own arithmetic.

Layout conventions the scorer rewards:
- Storage close to producers and to the market; production chains grouped (for example clay pit,
  brickyard, storage).
- Houses inside market, well, healer, school and chapel coverage; polluting industry away from houses.
- Walls around housing and storage; towers covering gates and approaches.
- Every building connected by road with the fewest tiles; existing roads are not a constraint.
